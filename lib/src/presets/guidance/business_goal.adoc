= Writing Business Goals

A `business_goal` item states a business outcome the organization wants to achieve.
It explains why the outcome matters, not the solution or work planned to achieve it.

== Traceability

A business goal is an upstream outcome; it does not need outgoing links to be useful.
In the `requirements-engineering-max` preset, a related `user_need` or `quality_attribute` may optionally point back to the goal using the configured inverse relation `is_motivated_by`.
This records that the business goal motivates the downstream item.
Check the project's traceability configuration for the roles and relations available in that project.
Do not invent role names, relation names, or target IDs.

== Guardrails

**Do not guess.** When information is missing or ambiguous, ask the user:

- Vague outcome → "What measurable change should the business see, and how would we know it happened?"
- Activity instead of outcome → "What business result should this work achieve?"
- Proposed solution instead of goal → "What outcome does this solution help achieve?"
- Missing business value → "Why does this outcome matter to the organization or its stakeholders?"
- Unclear scope or timeframe → "Which part of the business and what time horizon does this goal cover?"
- Missing baseline or target → "What is the current state, and what target would represent success?"

Never fabricate stakeholders, metrics, baselines, targets, timeframes, or traceability links.
If a measure cannot yet be quantified, describe how progress or achievement will be assessed and mark unresolved details as open questions.

== Creating a Business Goal

=== Step 1: Choose the Project's ID Convention

Use the ID prefix already established for business goals in the project.
If no convention exists, agree one before creating the item.

=== Step 2: Write the Item Block

[source,asciidoc]
----
[#<business-goal-id>, item, role=business_goal, title="<outcome> for <stakeholder>"]
--
<Stakeholder or organization> will <achieve a business outcome>.

Success is measured by <indicator>, from <baseline> to <target>, by <timeframe>.

<Why this outcome matters, if not clear from the outcome.>
--
----

== Optional Links to Business Goals

In the max preset, the relationship runs from a business goal to a downstream `user_need` or `quality_attribute` using `motivates`.
Its configured inverse is `is_motivated_by`, which lets the downstream item point back to the goal.

Add that inverse relation to a downstream item only when the goal genuinely motivates it:

[source,asciidoc]
----
[#<user-need-id>, item, role=user_need, title="<short title>"]
--
<User need statement.>

is_motivated_by:<business-goal-id>[]
--
----

This link is optional.
Omit it when no meaningful relationship exists, the goal ID is unknown, or the project does not configure the relation.
A business goal remains valid without links; never add a link just to satisfy the template.

`tracer:links[]` only displays relationships; it does not create them.
Add it to the business-goal item only when its incoming links should be displayed.
Otherwise omit it, including from the template above.

Include only measures and traceability links supported by the available information and configured relations.
Omit an unknown baseline, target, timeframe, or relation rather than making one up.

== Writing Rules

=== State the Outcome, Not the Work

Describe the change in business performance, capability, risk, or stakeholder value.
Keep planned features, implementation choices, and delivery activities in downstream items.

[cols="50,50"]
|===
| Wrong — activity or solution | Right — business outcome
| Build a customer analytics dashboard | Increase the share of renewals completed before expiry from 70% to 85% by the end of the year.
| Migrate reporting to a cloud platform | Reduce monthly reporting effort from 40 staff-hours to 20 by the end of Q2.
| Improve customer satisfaction | Raise the post-support satisfaction score from 3.8 to 4.3 out of 5 by the end of Q4.
|===

The examples are illustrative; use real stakeholder groups, baselines, targets, and timeframes for the project.

=== Make Success Assessable

Name an indicator and target when they are known.
State a baseline and timeframe when they are relevant and available.
Avoid unsupported precision: a plausible number is still fabricated if nobody supplied it.
If the outcome cannot be measured directly, state a credible way to assess progress or achievement.

=== Keep One Business Outcome

Keep one coherent business outcome in each item.
If the statement combines independent outcomes with "and", split them into separate goals.
Combining an outcome with its measure or rationale is fine.

=== Use Clear Business Language

Name the stakeholder or part of the organization affected when known.
Use concrete terms with a single interpretation.
Avoid vague claims such as "improve efficiency", "become world-class", or "increase value" unless the goal defines how success will be assessed.

== Reviewing a Business Goal

When reviewing, if the outcome is vague, unsupported, or written as a solution or activity, flag the issue with a specific question and suggested rewrite.
Do not invent missing information or silently rewrite the goal.

=== Quality Checklist

* [ ] States a business outcome, not a feature, implementation choice, or project activity.
* [ ] Identifies the affected stakeholder or organizational area when known.
* [ ] Explains the business value or makes it clear from the outcome.
* [ ] Defines a meaningful indicator and target where they are known and measurable.
* [ ] Includes baseline and timeframe where relevant and available; missing facts are identified rather than fabricated.
* [ ] Has one coherent outcome, with no unrelated goals bundled together.
* [ ] Uses specific, unambiguous language and avoids unsupported claims.
* [ ] Any optional relation uses a configured type and existing target ID; no link is added just to fill the template.
* [ ] Includes `tracer:links[]` only when incoming links to the business goal should be displayed.

=== If a Goal Is Vague or Solution-Led

. Identify whether the statement describes an outcome, activity, or proposed solution.
. Ask what observable business change is expected and why it matters.
. Ask for the indicator, baseline, target, and timeframe when applicable.
. Rewrite only with information the user provides; leave unknowns open.
. Put proposed features or delivery work in downstream user needs, use cases, or requirements.
